 MASTER POS SYSTEM SPECIFICATIONEnterprise Point-of-Sale & Business Operating SystemDocument Type: Master Product & Technical SpecificationSystem: Unified POS / Business Operating SystemArchitecture: Cloud + Offline-First + Event-DrivenPrimary Domains: POS, Payments, Commerce, Inventory, Customers, Employees, Finance, Analytics, AI, Hardware, Developer Platform1. SYSTEM VISIONThe system shall be designed as a Business Operating System for physical businesses, rather than as a conventional POS application.The POS is the transaction entry point. A completed transaction should automatically propagate through payment, inventory, customer management, employee attribution, accounting, analytics, marketing, and AI.The source architecture describes this as:Customer buys something → payment happens → inventory changes → employee gets credit → customer profile updates → accounting updates → analytics update → AI learns from the transaction.The ultimate platform hierarchy is:AI BRAIN    ↓BUSINESS OS    ↓SALES / PAYMENTS / INVENTORY / CUSTOMERS / EMPLOYEES    ↓COMMERCE HUB    ↓RESTAURANT / RETAIL / SERVICES ENGINES    ↓FINANCIAL OS    ↓DEVELOPER PLATFORM    ↓HARDWAREThis hierarchy is directly supported by the supplied architecture document.2. PRODUCT OBJECTIVESThe platform shall:Provide extremely fast POS checkout.Support multiple payment methods.Operate during internet outages.Maintain one unified order system.Maintain unified inventory across channels.Maintain a Customer 360 profile.Manage employees, roles, permissions and labor.Support retail, restaurant and service business models.Connect physical and digital commerce.Provide financial reconciliation and accounting capabilities.Provide enterprise multi-location management.Provide analytics and business intelligence.Provide an AI Business Copilot.Integrate with third-party applications.Support an extensible hardware ecosystem.Protect payment, customer and business data.3. OFFLINE TRANSACTION PROTOCOLEvery locally created transaction shall receive an immutable client-generated ID.transaction_id = UUIDdevice_id      = DEVICE UUIDsequence_no    = LOCAL SEQUENCEcreated_at     = DEVICE TIMESTAMPWhen synchronization occurs:CLIENT ↓SEND TRANSACTION ↓SERVER CHECKS TRANSACTION ID ↓ALREADY EXISTS? ├── YES → RETURN EXISTING RESULT └── NO  → PROCESS              ↓           COMMIT              ↓         ACKNOWLEDGEThis prevents duplicate transactions when a device retries after network failure.27. MASTER ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLESThe final implementation should be governed by these principles:1. POS is the transaction entry point, not the entire product.2. One order system across channels.3. One inventory system across locations and channels.4. Payments are their own controlled subsystem.5. Offline operation is fundamental, not an optional feature.6. Business events connect the domains.7. Sensitive operations are auditable.8. Customer data becomes Customer 360.9. Industry-specific workflows live in vertical engines.10. Hardware is abstracted from application logic.11. Analytics operates on unified business data.12. AI sits above governed business data and produces actionable intelligence.13. APIs and webhooks make the platform extensible.14. Financial records require durable, traceable transactions.15. Security and compliance are architectural requirements.16. Build the MVP around the transaction first; expand into the Business OS progressively.53.
